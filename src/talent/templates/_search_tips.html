Search tips
  • react python finds people who have both words (this is the default).
  • "machine learning" in quotes finds those words together.
  • python OR go (or python or go in small letters) finds people with either word.
  • python NEAR django finds people where the two words are close together (within 5 words, either order). python NEAR/3 django sets how close, from 1 to 50. Only a capital NEAR (or near/3) counts, so lives near boston still searches for the word near.
  • Every kind of search, including quotes, OR and NEAR, reads the same places: the profile, pipeline jobs, where they were found, and timeline notes.
  • c#, c++, f# and .net are words of their own, so c# does not find people who only know C or C++.
  • -recruiter or NOT manager leaves out people who have that exact word.
  • title:engineer, company:acme, location:sf, skill:python and source:referral narrow to one field. Punctuation stays, so skill:c++ and skill:node.js work, and skill:python OR go means either skill. Typing the same prefix twice, like company:google company:meta, means either one, and so does a prefix typed together with the same filter in More filters.
  • -title:manager or NOT source:referral leaves out people whose Title or Found via has it.
  • Example: title:engineer "machine learning" python OR go -recruiter. Brackets are ignored. Under the search box we show how your search was read. If a prefix is mistyped, like skil:python, you get a "Did you mean" link.
